Fact Checker

A 3D serious game about spotting fake news, made by a team of two across several university courses. The player is a journalist: over five days they read incoming news, check it on the computer and use a stamp to publish or reject it.

The game

Each day brings a pile of news. The player reads it, checks author, source and video on the computer and stamps it: green to publish, red to reject. When rejecting, they must say what is wrong: clickbait headline, inaccurate facts, opinion presented as fact, hidden advertising, old news, unreliable source, edited image or video.

At the end of the day they see their score and can review the items they got wrong.

What I did

  • I designed and wrote the news system: a data structure configured from the Unity editor, with every field needed to build a true or fake news item.
  • I proposed the gameplay and drew most of the user flows.
  • I made the cutscenes, with animation and dialogue, and the journalist's core gameplay.
  • I modelled some of the office's low-poly assets in Blender and animated the colleague with Mixamo.

How the news system works

Each news item is an asset: content writers create it from a Unity menu and fill in the fields in the Inspector, without touching code. The original assets are never changed during play.

Code

A news item as an asset

With [CreateAssetMenu] every news item becomes a project file. The solution lives inside the item: whether it should be published and, if fake, which errors it contains. The possible errors are an enum, so the stamp interface and the scoring use the same values. (Identifiers are in Italian: notizia means news item.)

Data kept apart from the player's choices

NotiziaRuntime wraps the asset and holds only the game state. At the end of the day scoring gives +1 for each right decision, +1 for each error spotted in a fake item and −1 for each wrong decision. Wrong items go into the review.
